1. Executive summary

  • The spreadsheet records 1,788 people across 10 generations from Fernando Umpad Muñasque: 1,331 blood descendants and 457 spouses or partners. The reconstructed tree gives each person a unique ID, a generation number, parents, spouse(s) and children, with a confidence label on every link.
  • The earliest named ancestors are Alejandro Muñasque and Saturnina Umpad, Fernando's parents. They appear only as a header line with no dates, so they are low confidence (named, but unverified).
  • Only one person in the whole file has dates: Emiliano Valiente Muñasque, born 20 May 1857, died 19 February 1940 aged 83. Everything else is undated. Generation-based birth estimates are given, clearly marked as estimates.
  • Fernando married Saturnina N. Valiente; their nine children (Emiliano, Agripina, Andrea, Marcos, Florintina, Epifanio, Sotero, Cosmiano and Cruz, all "Valiente Muñasque") head the nine branches of the tree. Branch sizes range from 16 to 390 recorded descendants.
  • The family is from Opon, Mactan Island, Cebu (today Lapu-Lapu City). By the 20th century most branches had moved to Mindanao: Lanao del Norte, the South Cotabato / General Santos / Sarangani settlement belt, North Cotabato, Agusan del Sur, Bukidnon, Misamis Occidental and Zamboanga del Sur, with smaller groups in Manila, the USA, Germany and Australia.
  • Independent corroboration was found for four descendants. Congressman Benhur Lago Salimbangon (1945–2020) is recorded by outside sources as the son of Martin Salimbangon and Milagros Lago, exactly as the spreadsheet has it. Fr. Rosalio N. Muñasque (died 2003) was a priest of the Diocese of Marbel. "Atty. Benjamin N. Muñasque" was Clerk of Court of the Court of First Instance of Cotabato in the 1970s. An evangelist "Sulpicio Y. Muñasque" reached General Santos from Lala, Lanao del Norte, on 7 March 1939, and a street there carries his name; he matches the spreadsheet's Sulpicio Ynot Muñasque.
  • No outside record was found for any of the first three generations. The 19th-century parish registers of Opon exist on FamilySearch (collection 5000324) but need a login; searching them is the single most valuable next step.
  • The compiler's generation labels and colours contain many slips. 133 rows were re-attached using the Filipino naming convention (a child's middle name is the mother's maiden surname) and the compiler's own branch sheets; every change is listed with its reason. 204 people remain flagged low confidence.

The spreadsheet is a derivative source: nobody's birth, baptism or marriage record is cited, and the compiler's own knowledge was clearly strongest for the living generations (4th to 8th) and weakest for the 19th century. What it proves is the compiler's understanding of who descends from whom. What it suggests, strongly, is the overall shape of the family and its geography. 3. How the tree was decoded

  1. Generation = font colour. The legend states black = 1st, dark red = 2nd, green = 3rd, blue = 4th, purple = 5th, orange = 6th, dark blue = 7th, red = 8th, light green = 9th generation. The text labels ("4G" etc.) in column F disagree with the colour on 70 rows; the colour was treated as primary because it is applied to every row, while labels are sparse and were visibly copied by hand.
  2. Spouse or child? A row is read as a spouse when it is indented deeper than the person above it and has the same colour (or a "#"/"2nd wife" marker). A row one colour deeper is a child. The same colour at normal indent is a sibling.
  3. Naming-convention check. In Philippine usage since the American period the middle name (or initial) is the mother's maiden surname. For every child the middle name was compared with the proposed mother's surname: 1094 links pass, 36 fail, and the rest cannot be tested (no middle name, or a single-name spouse). A failure usually means the compiler listed a person under a sibling instead of under the parents; 133 such rows were moved, each with its reason recorded in overrides.csv, and the branch sheets (S2) were checked for agreement in every case.
  4. Additional partners. The rows "#2 Faustina Ynot" and "#3 Basilia" after Marcos's descendants, and "# Sarip Arabia Omar" after Cosmiano's, are read as later partners of Marcos and Cosmiano respectively, because their children are coloured 3rd generation and because the compiler's own branch sheets (Sheet5, Sheet8) put them inside those branches. An alternative reading, that they were later wives of Fernando himself, cannot be excluded but is less consistent with the colours.
  5. Verification pass. After the registers were built, an automatic check confirmed: every parent-child link is reciprocal and advances the generation by exactly one; every spouse pair is at the same generation; there are no cycles; every one of the 1,794 name rows is accounted for (8 duplicates dropped); and all 977 names that could be matched between the branch sheets and the master sheet fall inside the branch the compiler assigned them to. Two anomalies were found and fixed (three of Rosalia A. Muñasque's children mis-read as spouses; "Lourdes Salili" entered as the wife of two men, logged). Dated statements were then fact-checked by a second research pass (§6).
  6. Confidence labels. Moderate = the spreadsheet's placement is internally consistent and the naming check passes or is untestable. Low = a naming-check failure, a colour or indent anomaly, a conflict between the two sheets, or a header-only entry. High is reserved for facts with two independent outside sources (only Benhur Salimbangon's dates qualify). Nothing in the first three generations rises above moderate.

4. The reconstructed lineage

4.1 Generation 0–1: the origin at Opon

Alejandro Muñasque and Saturnina Umpad (G0) are named once, in the header of the master sheet, as the parents of Fernando. Umpad (also spelled Ompad) is one of the most common surnames of Lapu-Lapu City today, which fits an Opon origin. No dates, no other children. Low confidence until a baptism or marriage record for Fernando is found.

Fernando Umpad Muñasque (G1) married Saturnina N. Valiente. Because their son Emiliano was born in 1857, Fernando was probably born about 1825–1835 and married about 1853–1856: the earliest census measure of Philippine marriage age (1903) puts women at about 21 and men a few years older (S45), so a father in 1857 was most likely 22–30; an 1820 birth is possible if Emiliano was not his first child. Nothing else is known. The surname Muñasque is rare (about 1,050 bearers, mostly in Central Visayas and Mindanao, per Forebears) and is not a known Spanish word; the family story (S3) that it was made from the names Mukilan, Anañas and Roque has no documentary trace. The second research round opened an open transcription of the 1849 Catálogo alfabético de apellidos (S53): the exact spelling Muñasque is not in it, but page 87 of the reprint, among the Moñ- names, has an entry read by three of six readings as "moñasque" (the others "monasque", "moliasque"), between "monasca" and "moñascas". If the page image confirms Moñasque, the family name is very probably a catalogue surname taken in 1850 and later written with a u, which is a common Visayan o/u interchange. Umpad, Ynot, Pino and Valiente are catalogue names too; Cosip, Gusat, Salimbangon and Patalinghug are not, which marks them as older indigenous Mactan names. This is a lead, not a proof: the reading is marked "best effort" by the transcriber and must be checked on the scan (Google Books copy, p. 87; Issuu copy, p. 121).

4.3 Branch summaries

Emiliano Valiente Muñasque (1857–1940) and Irenea Gusat. The largest branch (390 recorded descendants). Six children: Leona (m. Juan Dumo), Godofreda (m. Blas Cosip), Asisclo (m. Felicitas Gonzales), Diosdado (m. Maxima Lucero), Francisco (m. Cesaria Ancajas) and Sergia (m. Quintin Salimbangon Sr.). Godofreda's Cosip children and grandchildren are the Opon core of the sheet (Patalinghug, Lara, Rivera, Zambra, Dueñas lines; residences Opon, Glan, Koronadal, Polomolok, General Santos). Asisclo's children settled in Lanao del Norte, Polomolok and Agusan del Sur; one daughter lives in Germany. Sergia's Salimbangon children include Dr. Quintin Muñasque Salimbangon Jr. The compiler had placed Czar, Ruth and Dr. Quintin Jr. under their sister Thelma; their middle name "Muñasque" shows they are Sergia's children, and Sheet2 agrees. Confidence: moderate for the structure; the Gusat surname is itself rare in Philippine data (Forebears), so Irenea's family remains a lead.

Agripina Valiente Muñasque and Ambrosio Salimbangon. Ten children: Sabiana (m. Ciriaco Go), Teodoro (m. Petronila Almazon), Teodulfo (m. Felipa Gelig), Felix (m. Estelita Esorio), Martin (m. Milagros Lago), Vecing (m. 1 Julio Tumulak, 2 Vicente Mejares), Pulguera (m. Sulficio Basmayor), Felicisimo (m. Pascuala Yap), Rosita (m. Marciano Mondigo) and Antonio (m. Lucia Ikong). 185 recorded descendants. Martin's family lived in Curva, Medellin, in northern Cebu, where Martin was a schoolteacher and barangay captain; his son Benhur became Congressman for Cebu's 4th District (2007–2019) and was buried at Mactan Island Memorial Garden, Lapu-Lapu, a cemetery run by his nephew. His brother Edwin was elected mayor of Medellin in 2025. Outside sources confirm the Martin–Milagros–Benhur chain but say nothing about Agripina or Ambrosio, so the link from Martin back to Opon still rests on the spreadsheet alone. The compiler's "Cong. Benhur" entry gives "# Janice Zamora" as a second partner; that is Janice Zamora Salimbangon, his widow and successor in Congress. Confidence: moderate; the Martin–Benhur link moderate-to-high.

Andrea Valiente Muñasque and Leon Pino. Seven children (Juan, Demetrio "Etiok", Leonardo, Restituto, Sergio and others), 106 recorded descendants, residence not stated in the sheets. Sergio Muñasque Pino had two partners (Benavintora Etcuban; "# 2 Gregoria Arnoco"), which the sheet marks explicitly. Leonardo, Restituto and Sergio were listed by the compiler under Demetrio; their middle name "Muñasque" and surname Pino identify them as Andrea's children, and Sheet4 lists them at the top level. Confidence: moderate.

Marcos Valiente Muñasque with Matilde ("Matildi") Perez, and later partners Faustina Ynot and Basilia. By Matilde: Purification (m. Renato Espinosa), Coronacion (m. Jesus Cabahug), Palexina (m. Antonio Serra Ty) and Col. Coronado "Stim" Perez Muñasque (m. Narcessa Bondoc Vivera; later Linda Jimenez). By Faustina Ynot: Leonarda (m. Tiburcio Booc), Gregoria (m. Steve Mendoza), Fernando Y. (m. Candida Sajonia). By Basilia: Antonio (m. Gavina Booc), Timotea (m. Alipio Bontilao), Gavino. 154 recorded descendants. The Cabahug and Ty lines look urban (Cebu/Manila professional families; one Ty married a Murakami) but no residences are given. No outside record of Col. Coronado Muñasque was found. Confidence: moderate for Matilde's line; moderate for the later partners being Marcos's rather than Fernando's.

Florintina Valiente Muñasque and Fruto Etcuban. Five children: Benvienido (m. Dolores Tabernero), Maximo, Federica (m. Pedro Mendoza), Adelfa and Leoncio (m. Desederia Booc). 53 recorded descendants. Cosmiano Valiente Muñasque with Sofia Montesclaros Mansueto, and a second family with Sarip Arabia Omar. By Sofia: Marcelina (m. Walter Cebedo Sr.) and Fernando M. Muñasque Sr. (m. By Sarip Arabia Omar: Hadja Amina Omar Muñasque, who married Toncio Maulana Tambingag; their nine children and the Pangolima, Hadjan, Hassan, Moraga and Marohombsar in-laws are a Muslim (Maranao/Maguindanao-named) family. The Tambingag surname clusters today in General Santos City and Sarangani (Forebears; a 2025 Maasim council candidate). 104 recorded descendants. Children of Malapat Tambingag and Tarhata Marohombsar carry the initial T. for the mother's given name, a Moro usage, which the naming check correctly flags but which is not an error. Confidence: moderate for Sofia's line; low-to-moderate for the Omar/Tambingag family.

Cruz Valiente Muñasque and Remedios Dela Piña. Two children, Elmo and Mariano (m. Burgos); 16 recorded descendants, including a Matthews family (Mariano's grandson Frank Luis Fernando married Patricia Matthews). Elmo is probably the Cebu building contractor ("Galan and Muñasque") whose 1966–67 contract with Tropical Commercial Co. was litigated to the Supreme Court in 1985. Confidence: moderate.

5. Ancestor and key-person profiles

Fernando Umpad Muñasque (G1, progenitor). Son of Alejandro Muñasque and Saturnina Umpad; husband of Saturnina N. Valiente; father of nine. Born about 1820–1835 (estimate from Emiliano's 1857 birth); of Opon, Cebu. No image, record or date exists for him. The Umpad/Ompad surname is a Mactan Island name, which supports the Opon origin. Sources: S1, S33.

Saturnina N. Valiente (G1). Wife of Fernando; mother of the nine "Valiente Muñasque" children. Her middle initial N. is the only clue to her own mother's surname. Valiente is a Spanish surname that also appears in the 1849 Claveria catalogue tradition, but her family is undocumented. Sources: S1.

Emiliano Valiente Muñasque (G2), 20 May 1857 – 19 Feb 1940. The only dated person. Husband of Irenea Gusat; father of six; head of the largest branch. Born under Spanish rule and died a year before the Pacific War, aged 83. His death in February 1940 should be in the Opon civil register (Act 3753 was in force) and the Opon parish burial book; neither has been searched yet. Sources: S1.

Agripina Valiente Muñasque and Ambrosio Salimbangon (G2). Parents of the Salimbangon branch (ten children). The branch later appears in Curva, Medellin, 120 km north of Opon, where the son Martin was a teacher and village head; how and when the family moved is unknown. The Cebuano word salimbangon is a plant name (Wiktionary). Sources: S1, S14, S15.

Martin Salimbangon and Milagros Lago (G3). Schoolteacher and barangay captain of Curva, Medellin (S14, S15); Milagros born 8 September 1915 or 1916, still living in 2013 at 98 (S16, S17). Parents of Aida, Ofelia/Ophelia (m. Jose Cue; d. before 2006), Benhur, Cesar, Mariquita (m. Carlos Yeung), Danilo, Edwin (Mayor of Medellin 2025) and Freddie per the spreadsheet; the press counts seven children.

Congressman Benhur Lago Salimbangon (G4), 3 Jun 1945 – 24 Dec 2020. Provincial Board Member 1992–1998; Representative for Cebu's 4th District, elected 2007 (annulled on protest), re-elected 2010, serving to 2019; married Mariter Trocino and later Janice Zamora, who succeeded him in Congress; seven children including Francis Edward (Mayor of Tabogon). Died of lung cancer; buried at Mactan Island Memorial Garden, Lapu-Lapu City. The only person in the tree whose dates are confirmed by two independent outside sources. Sources: S10–S13.

Sulpicio Ynot Muñasque (G3). Son of Sotero and Sebirina Ynot. Very probably the evangelist "Sulpicio Y. Munasque" of the United Evangelical Church who came from Lala, Lanao del Norte, to Dadiangas on 7 March 1939, eight days after the first NLSA settlers landed, and who with his wife petitioned Gen. Paulino Santos for a church lot in 1941; a street in Dadiangas North bears his name. Married Arcadia N. Acaso; father of Gideon A. Muñasque Sr. Sources: S1, S29, S30, S40.

Rev. Fr. Rosalio N. Muñasque (G4), d. 2 Dec 2003. Son of Basilio Ynot Muñasque and Rosalia V. Nuñeza. Diocesan priest of Marbel (Koronadal, South Cotabato): rector of the Our Lady of Perpetual Help Seminary about 1975, parish priest of Banga 1975–1978, spiritual director of Notre Dame of Kiamba. Sources: S21–S24.

Atty. Benjamin N. Muñasque (G4). Brother of Fr. Rosalio; married Erlinda Yu. A Supreme Court decision of 31 October 1974 (A.M. No. P-145) names "Benjamin N. Muñasque, Clerk of Court of the Court of First Instance in Cotabato City" and records his complaint of 2 February 1972 against a stenographer; the rare surname, the initial N. and the title make the identification probable. Sources: S1, S25.

Col. Coronado "Stim" Perez Muñasque (G3). Son of Marcos and Matilde Perez; married Narcessa Bondoc Vivera and later Linda Jimenez; grandfather of Stimson Muñasque. The title Col. comes from Sheet5 only; no military record was found. Sources: S1, S2.

Hadja Amina Omar Muñasque (G3). Daughter of Cosmiano by Sarip Arabia Omar; wife of Toncio Maulana Tambingag; mother of Bai, Pompie, Udtog, Hadja Alijah Patring, Eduardo, Rachima, Dayang, Solaiman and Malapat. The honorific Hadja indicates she made the pilgrimage to Mecca. The family is the one Muslim branch of the tree; its location (probably the General Santos–Sarangani coast) is inferred from the surname's distribution, not from the sheet. Sources: S1, S2.

Atty. Marnito B. Cosep / Cosip (G5). Son of Hermenio Muñasque Cosip and Marina Berami; Provincial Legal Officer of South Cotabato, Koronadal City. Sources: S2, S27, S28.

7. Geography and migration

The branch sheets (S2) give a residence for 72 people, almost all in generations 4–6, so the map shows where the family was living around 2014–2020, not where each person was born. Counts by place: lanao 17, opon 11, polomolok 8, gensan 6, manila 5, glan 4, koronadal 2, stonino 2, rosario 2, upi 1, trento 1, midsayap 1, cebu 1, cavite 1, lebak 1, surallah 1, tacurong 1, bukidnon 1, plaridel 1, pagadian 1; abroad: Usa 3, Germany 1, Australia 1. Only one move is dated and documented: Sulpicio Y. Muñasque from Lala, Lanao del Norte, to Dadiangas (General Santos) on 7 March 1939 (S29). The Opon origin of every other branch is the compiler's statement (S3) supported by the Mactan surnames (Umpad, Patalinghug, Booc, Ynot, Etcuban) in the early generations. The Martin Salimbangon family's home in Curva, Medellin (northern Cebu) comes from the press (S14–S16). All other arrows on the map are therefore hypothetical routes between a documented origin and a documented later residence.

Historical context that explains the pattern: Opon was a poor-soil fishing and boat-building town (population 14,851 in 1903, 33,426 in 1939; S37, S38). From 1913 the colonial government planted agricultural colonies in Cotabato: Pikit was founded on 17 June 1913 by about 120 Cebuano colonists, and Glan ("Colony No. 9") in 1914 by Cebu pioneers (S39). The National Land Settlement Administration (Commonwealth Act 441, 1939) opened the Koronadal and Allah valleys; its first settlers landed at Sarangani Bay on 27 February 1939 under Gen. Paulino Santos, who had been running the project since January (S40), eight days before Sulpicio Muñasque arrived there (S29). Post-war programmes (LASEDECO 1950, NARRA 1954, EDCOR in Kapatagan, Lanao) kept the flow going. The family's Lanao del Norte, Polomolok, Koronadal, General Santos and Glan clusters sit exactly on these settlement fronts.

10. Research gaps and ranked next steps

Ranked by expected value and feasibility.

  1. Opon parish registers, 1840–1940 (highest value). FamilySearch collection 5000324, "Philippines, Central Visayas, Church Records, 1737–2005", holds baptisms, marriages and burials for Cebu parishes, partly indexed, images browsable with a free account; the FamilySearch wiki inventory lists the parish Nuestra Señora de la Virgen de Regla, Lapu-Lapu City (S34, S35). Search surname Muñasque / Munasque / Muñasqui with place Opon, then browse the unindexed images by year. Targets: Emiliano's baptism (May–June 1857) naming Fernando and Saturnina Valiente and the grandparents; Fernando's marriage to Saturnina (before 1857); Fernando's own baptism (c. 1820–1835) naming Alejandro and Saturnina Umpad; baptisms of the other eight children (c. 1858–1885); Emiliano's burial (February 1940). One hit would settle the first three generations.
  2. Emiliano's civil death record, Feb 1940. Request a "Transcription of Facts of Death from the Registry Book" (Civil Registry Form 2A) from the Lapu-Lapu City Local Civil Registry Office; PSA itself holds almost nothing before 1945 (S36). Also check FamilySearch civil-registration collections 2018411 (1902–1945) and 1410394 (local, 1888–1986).
  3. 1849 Claveria catalogue. Open the Filipinas Heritage Library scan (biblio/50226) or the 1973 National Archives reprint and check the "Mu-" pages for Muñasque. Presence would mean the surname was assigned in 1850 and the Mukilan–Anañas–Roque story is folk etymology; absence would favour an older indigenous or invented name.
  4. Spanish-era padrones / vecindarios. FamilySearch collection 5000268, "Philippines, Neighborhood Census, 1836–1899", extracts parish household lists; a Muñasque household in Opon would give ages and relationships for Fernando's generation.
  5. Benhur Salimbangon's own documents. His House of Representatives profile or obituary programme would state parents and birthplace and resolve Medellin vs Cebu City. Ask the family for Milagros Lago's death notice to fix her birth year.
  6. Salimbangon move to Medellin. Search the Medellin parish (San Isidro Labrador?) and civil registers for Martin Salimbangon's marriage to Milagros Lago (c. 1935–1945) and for Ambrosio Salimbangon; this would show whether Agripina's family moved north or whether Ambrosio was from Medellin.
  7. Diocese of Marbel records for Fr. Rosalio N. Muñasque (ordination file, necrology) and Court of First Instance of Cotabato appointment records for Atty. Benjamin N. Muñasque: both would name parents and birthplace and tie the Sotero line to Opon with a document.
  8. UCCP Lagao history (S29) in full and the UCCP/United Evangelical Church archives for Sulpicio Y. Muñasque: his ordination or appointment papers would give his birthplace and parents (expected: Opon; Sotero and Sebirina Ynot).
  9. Interview the compiler. The spreadsheet's author (file saved by "MarkhyNet") interviewed Amador Piligro in 2006 and clearly knew the Cosip/Patalinghug families of Opon personally. Ask for the sources of the 1857 and 1940 dates, the meaning of the colour and "#" conventions, and the sex of each person (needed for a correct GEDCOM).
  10. Gusat, Ynot, Etcuban, Cosip families of Opon. These in-law surnames are rare and local; FamilySearch index hits for them in Opon would indirectly confirm the Muñasque entries around them.
  11. DNA. No DNA data was supplied. Autosomal tests of descendants from at least three different branches (e.g. Emiliano's, Sotero's and Cosmiano's) would test whether the nine branches really share Fernando and Saturnina as ancestors; Y-DNA from male-line Muñasques would test whether the surname has a single origin. Results give relationship ranges, not exact positions, and cannot by themselves distinguish Fernando from a brother.
  12. Lower priority: Col. Coronado Muñasque in Philippine Army/Constabulary rosters; Dr. Ricardo C. Muñasque Jr. in PRC physician lists; Engr. Marlon Cosep in PRC engineer lists; the Tambingag family through Sarangani local records; US/Australian records for the Muñasques abroad.

13. Research conclusion

Established (moderate confidence): the descent of about 1,300 named people from Fernando Umpad Muñasque and Saturnina Valiente of Opon through nine children; the internal structure of each branch; the family's move from Mactan to the Mindanao settlement areas during the 20th century; the identities and public careers of Benhur Salimbangon, Fr. Rosalio Muñasque, Atty. Benjamin Muñasque, Atty. Marnito Cosep and the evangelist Sulpicio Muñasque.

Probable but unverified: the dates of Emiliano (1857–1940); that Faustina Ynot and Basilia were later partners of Marcos (not of Fernando); that Elmo D. Muñasque is the 1966 Cebu contractor.

Unverified: Alejandro Muñasque and Saturnina Umpad as Fernando's parents; the probable "Moñasque" entry in the 1849 catalogue as the origin of the surname; every birth, marriage and death in generations 1–4 other than Emiliano's; the Amador Piligro surname story; the whole Tambingag (Muslim) branch beyond the spreadsheet.

Cannot currently be determined: anything before Alejandro; whether Muñasque is a Claveria-catalogue surname; where and when each branch left Opon. All of these are answerable from the Opon parish registers and the Cebu civil registers once those collections are searched with a FamilySearch account or at the Lapu-Lapu City civil registry.
